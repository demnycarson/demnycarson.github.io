How to Rank Higher on Google Maps (A Simple Guide for Local Businesses)

By Carson Demny · October 28, 2026 · 6 min read

When someone searches for a service “near me,” Google shows a map with a short list of businesses at the top. That list is where most of the calls go. The good news: getting there isn't magic. It comes down to a few things Google openly says it looks at, plus steady work every month.

What Google looks at

Google says local results are based mainly on three things:

  • Relevance: how well your listing matches what the person searched for.
  • Distance: how far your business is from the person searching (or the area they searched).
  • Prominence: how well-known and trusted your business is, which includes reviews, your website and how you show up across the web.

You can't move your building, so distance is mostly out of your hands. But relevance and prominence are things you can improve, and that's where most businesses leave easy wins on the table.

Step 1: Get your Google Business Profile 100% complete

Your Google Business Profile is your listing on Google Maps. A half-finished profile is one of the most common reasons businesses get buried. Make sure you have:

  • The right main category (be specific: “Plumber,” not just “Contractor”), plus any extra categories that truly fit.
  • Your services listed out, with short descriptions.
  • Accurate hours, phone number and website.
  • Your service areas if you travel to customers.
  • Real photos of your work, your team and your vehicles or location.
  • A clear business description that says what you do and where.

Don't stuff keywords or city names into your business name. Google's rules say your name should match your real-world name, and breaking that can get your profile suspended.

Step 2: Make your website back it up

Google checks your website to understand what you do. A strong site helps your Maps ranking:

  • A page for each main service, so Google (and customers) can see exactly what you offer.
  • Your name, address or service area, and phone shown the same way as on your Google profile.
  • Pages for the cities you serve, written with real local details, not copy-paste.
  • Fast loading on phones, since most local searches happen on a phone.

Step 3: Get a steady flow of reviews

Reviews help with prominence, and they're what customers read before they call. The key word is steady: a few new reviews every month beats a burst once a year.

  • Ask every happy customer, right after the job, with a direct link to your review page.
  • Reach back out to past customers who never left one.
  • Reply to every review, good or bad, politely and professionally.

Step 4: Keep your profile active

An active profile signals a real, open business. Each month:

  • Post an update (a recent job, a seasonal tip or an offer).
  • Add a few new photos.
  • Answer any questions people ask on your listing.
  • Check that your hours and info are still correct, especially around holidays.

How long does it take?

It depends on your location and how competitive your industry is. Many businesses see movement within a few months of consistent work. Be careful with anyone who promises a guaranteed #1 spot: nobody controls Google's results, and honest work over time is what moves you up.
